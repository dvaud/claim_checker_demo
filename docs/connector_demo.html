The ENLENS connector: a recorded demo

Claude checks claims against your own documents and does the reading and grading on your Claude plan. The connector, running on your computer, keeps the projects, picks the passages, checks every quote against the source and keeps the history.

What you are seeing. A recorded run on 27 October 2026.
  • Claude's part was played by Claude Opus 5.5 in a test harness. The harness starts the real connector over MCP (stdio), the protocol the Claude desktop app uses.
  • The user messages were scripted. The tool calls, results and Claude's replies are as recorded; passages are shortened here.
  • All grades are Claude's own, and every quote passed the connector's check on the first try.
  • Sources: the pilot's 38 documents, linked as the project Pilot, and a 6-document zip turned into a new project, Sample.
Setting it up on your computer 
pip install './enlens-claims-0.4.1[gui,connector]'
enlens-mcp --install-claude-desktop     # adds the connector to the Claude desktop app's settings
# quit and reopen the Claude app. For Claude Code: enlens-mcp --print-config shows the command

What the demo shows, and what it does not

  • Works as intended. In this chat:
    • a project was created from a zip;
    • a supported claim (leakage), an unsupported one (climate plans) and a contradicted one (Uruguay water) were checked;
    • Claude read further and searched again with other words;
    • the history was listed.
    Claude flagged the unreadable file, separated weak evidence from support, and pointed out that the Uruguay contradiction rests on one government source.
  • Keyword search still misses evidence. The pilot's own check found that IRENA (2021, Decarbonising end-use sectors, p. 17) states that "fewer than one-fifth of countries have put forward national hydrogen strategies", which bears on the climate plans claim. Here the keyword ranking placed that passage 22nd, so 24 passages over three rounds did not reach it. "Nothing found" means nothing in the passages read, not nothing in the sources. The fix I would add next is a meaning-based search, using an embedding model that also runs locally in Ollama.
  • No "partly refutes" grade. Passages that lean against a claim without stating the opposite go ungraded. The same rule held in the pilot.
