Policy brief: hybrid working at the Municipality of Northbridge (fictional), 2025

Background. Since 2022 municipal staff may work from home up to three days a week. This brief summarises what the municipality learned in the first three years, for other public employers considering the same step.

Staff wellbeing. In the 2024 staff survey, 78 percent of staff said hybrid working improved their work-life balance. Sick leave fell from 6.1 to 5.4 percent. Staff under 30 asked for more team days at the office, and several teams that had lost contact with each other during the first year introduced fixed team days on their own initiative.

Recruitment. Vacancies were filled faster than before: the average time to fill a position fell from 14 to 10 weeks. Applicants named the possibility of working from home as one of the main reasons to apply.

Office space. The municipality closed one of its three office buildings, which reduced energy costs and freed the building for social housing.

Services to citizens. Citizens did not report longer waiting times or lower quality of service. Complaints about the municipality fell slightly in 2023 and 2024. Counter services were never offered from home and were not affected.

Points of attention. Managers found it harder to notice when staff were overloaded. The municipality now trains managers to hold regular one-to-one conversations, online or in person.

Recommendation. Keep hybrid working, and introduce one fixed team day a week for every department.
