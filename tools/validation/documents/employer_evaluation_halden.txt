Two years of the Two Days Home policy: internal evaluation Halden Mutual Insurance (fictional), Human Resources and Operations, 2025

Background. Halden Mutual Insurance (fictional) employs about 1,900 people in Rivermouth and Eastgate. In January 2023 it introduced a policy that lets staff in claims, customer service and administration work from home two days a week, with Wednesday as a fixed office day for every team. This evaluation covers January 2023 to December 2024 and was written for the executive board.

Method. We compared 2022 with 2024 using company records on claims handled, error rates, resignations and floor space, and a questionnaire answered by 1,214 employees. No comparison group outside the company was available, so some of the changes reported below may reflect the labour market rather than the policy. The questionnaire was anonymous and open for three weeks in November 2024.

Productivity. Claims handled per full-time employee rose by 6 percent between 2022 and 2024, and the share of claims returned for correction stayed at 3 percent. Team leaders attributed the rise to uninterrupted blocks of work at home. Output in customer service, which depends on shared queues, was unchanged. The figures exclude staff on long-term leave.

Resignations. The annual resignation rate fell from 14 percent in 2022 to 9 percent in 2024. In the questionnaire, 61 percent of staff said that the policy was an important reason to stay with the company. The evaluation notes that the job market also weakened over the same period, so the fall cannot be put down to the policy alone.

Hiring. Applications per vacancy rose from 18 to 27, and 58 percent of new hires in 2024 named home working as a reason for applying. Recruiters noted that candidates from outside the region, who would not have considered a daily commute to Rivermouth, now applied in larger numbers. The recruitment team also noted fewer questions about the location of the job.

Collaboration. Teams whose members did not share an office day had more trouble arranging meetings. In the questionnaire, 44 percent of employees in such teams said that decisions take longer than before, against 15 percent in teams that share a day. Wednesday became the busiest day of the week for meetings. Several teams have since moved their meetings to the shared day.

Office space. Halden gave up one of its two floors in the Eastgate building and cut the area per employee from 11 to 7 square metres. Savings on rent and heating were about 14 percent of the property budget. The company will not renew the lease on its second Rivermouth car park, which it no longer needs. The move was completed in the summer of 2024.

New staff. Employees with less than one year of service were allowed only one home day a week. Their questionnaire answers were the least positive: 39 percent said that they had enough opportunities to learn from colleagues, against 71 percent of staff with more than three years of service. Some new employees asked for more office days.

Staff satisfaction. In the questionnaire, 83 percent said that the policy improved their balance between work and private life, and sick leave fell from 5.9 to 5.2 percent. Fewer employees reported being tired at the end of the working day than in 2022. Staff in customer service were the most positive group. Response rates were similar across departments.

Management. Eleven of the 46 team leaders said that they find it harder to judge the performance of staff they see less often. The company has started to evaluate staff on agreed targets instead of on presence, and offers a course for team leaders on leading hybrid teams. Not every team leader has attended the course, and the company plans to make it compulsory from 2026.

Conclusion. The board is advised to keep the policy, to keep Wednesday as a fixed office day for every team, and to review the rules for staff in their first year. The evaluation does not claim that the policy caused every change, but none of the measures it tracks moved in the wrong direction over the two years. The board will discuss the evaluation in its autumn meeting.
