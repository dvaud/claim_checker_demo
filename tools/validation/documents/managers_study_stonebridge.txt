Managing what you cannot see: interviews with 31 middle managers Department of Organisation Studies, University of Stonebridge (fictional), working paper, 2025

Background. Hybrid working has changed what it means to manage a team. Earlier research looked mostly at employees; this study asks their managers. We interviewed 31 middle managers in banking, local government and manufacturing in the Rivermouth region between March and July 2024. All of them had led teams both before and after the pandemic.

Method. Interviews lasted between 45 and 80 minutes and were recorded and transcribed. Two researchers coded the transcripts independently and resolved disagreements by discussion. The sample is small and not representative: managers who agreed to be interviewed may be more interested in the subject than others, and we did not interview any employees about the same teams.

Visibility. Most managers said that they had to give up judging by presence. Twenty-two of the 31 now agree written targets with each employee, and several said that this made evaluations fairer. Eight managers admitted that they had, at least once, given a better assessment to an employee who was often in the office than to a remote colleague with the same results.

Trust. Managers who had known their staff before the pandemic said that trust had survived the move home. Managers of teams formed after 2020 found trust harder to build: 11 of the 14 such managers said that it took more than a year before they felt that they knew their staff. Several said that they relied on regular video calls to keep contact with newer staff.

Monitoring. Four managers used software that logs activity, all at the request of their employer. Three of the four said that it told them little about the quality of work and that employees disliked it. None had used the data to discipline anyone, and two had stopped looking at it after a few weeks. The other managers had decided against such software.

Overload. Managers said that they found it harder to see when employees were overloaded. Signs they used to notice in the corridor, such as tired faces or late departures, were invisible on a screen. Fifteen managers had started weekly one-to-one calls for this reason, which they thought worked well. The calls usually take about twenty minutes.

Teams. Managers described a loss of informal contact between team members. Problems that used to be solved by a question across the desk now needed a message or a call, and some were never raised. Managers who fixed team days at the office thought that the day was worth the journey for the whole team. Several said that this loss was the hardest to explain to senior management.

Performance. Most managers believed that their teams were as productive as before, and nine believed that they were more productive. None had measured it. They said that the quantity of work was easy to see but that creativity and the quality of ideas were not. Three managers said that they would like their employer to provide measures that could be compared.

New staff. Managers worried most about employees in their first two years. They said that these employees asked fewer questions, and that mistakes were found later. Eighteen managers had introduced buddy systems; they could not say whether these made up for the loss of learning at the next desk. Two managers allowed new employees extra office days.

Managers themselves. Sixteen managers said that they now worked more hours than before, mostly on calls in the evening to reach staff on different schedules. Six reported symptoms that they described as exhaustion, and two had been on sick leave for it in the previous year. Only three of them had been offered any support by their employer.

Conclusion. Managing hybrid teams is not harder or easier, but different: it asks for written targets, regular conversations and planned contact. Managers who did these things reported fewer problems. The study cannot say whether the teams performed better, because it relied on managers' accounts alone. Further research should include employees as well.
