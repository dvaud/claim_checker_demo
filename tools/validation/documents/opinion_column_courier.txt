Back to the office, please Opinion by Marta Hollin, The Lowland Courier (fictional), 8 June 2025

Opening. Every Monday morning I walk past the glass tower on Mill Street and count the lit windows. This week there were eleven, out of some four hundred. The tower was built for two thousand people, and the company that rents it has told its staff to come in when it suits them. It rarely seems to suit anyone, and the lift attendant has been moved to the post room.

Culture. Offices were never only about desks. They are where young people learn how to disagree politely, how to ask for a rise and whom to ask when the spreadsheet breaks. A generation that starts its career in a bedroom will have to learn all this from a screen, and I doubt that it will. I would sooner see employers insist on two shared days a week for anyone in their first three years.

Loneliness. Readers write to me every week to say that they have not seen a colleague for months. I am told that this is a minor complaint, a luxury of the well paid. I do not think that the people who write to me find it minor, and I know that a columnist's postbag is not a study, but it is the only evidence I have. Some of them are under thirty and live alone.

Productivity. Managers tell me over lunch that nothing gets done on home days. They cannot prove it, and the studies I have glanced at say something more mixed, but I know what I see in my own team: the good ideas arrive in the corridor, not in the video call. Perhaps that is only nostalgia. I would still rather not find out the hard way.

Commuting. Defenders of home working say that nobody misses the commute. I miss mine. The twenty minutes on the tram were the only twenty minutes of the day when nobody could reach me, and I did my best thinking there. I accept that this is an unusual view, and that for people with an hour each way it would be absurd. Ask me again in winter, when the tram is warm and the street is not.

Climate. We are told that home working is good for the planet. Perhaps it is. But a half-empty office still needs heating, and a home needs heating too, and the sums that would settle the matter are rarely shown. I would be glad to be proved wrong by someone with a spreadsheet and the patience to count the second car trips.

The high street. The cost to the city is already visible. Shops near the office towers have closed, the sandwich bar on Mill Street among them, and the owner of the tower has cut its rents by a fifth to keep its tenants. A town centre built around five days of commuters cannot easily live on two. Nobody calls that a measured finding, but anyone who has walked there will recognise it.

Employers. Employers who force everyone back for four days will lose their best people to those who do not. I have seen it happen twice among my friends. Recruitment in this market is a contest, and the offer of two days at home is worth more to most candidates than a small rise, whatever the personnel department believes. Some of them are very good employers.

Managers. Good managers do not need to see their staff to know whether they are working. Bad managers need to see them all the time. Home working has simply made the difference visible, and a surprising number of bosses have discovered which kind they are. I do not envy them, and I do not pity them either. The rest will need to learn quickly.

Evenings. I notice that my colleagues who work from home answer e-mails at ten at night. Nobody asked them to. Perhaps the real problem is that the home has become the office, and the office has become a place that we visit. A day that never ends is not a perk, whatever the brochure says about flexibility. I check mine as well, which is not a defence.

Verdict. My modest proposal is three days in the office for everyone, the same three days, and no exceptions for people who think that they are too senior. I expect to be called old-fashioned. I have been called worse by better people, and the lit windows on Mill Street would be a pleasant sight. I will keep counting windows in the meantime and will report any change.
