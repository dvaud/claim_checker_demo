Working from home and health: a review of 5,412 occupational health consultations Rivermouth Occupational Health Service (fictional), annual report, 2025

Background. The Rivermouth Occupational Health Service (fictional) provides health checks, sickness advice and rehabilitation to about 120 employers. Since 2021 its physicians have recorded the usual place of work of each patient. This report uses those records from 2021 to 2024 and describes what changed for employees who work from home.

Method. We analysed 5,412 consultations and 1,860 sickness absence episodes. Employees were grouped by the number of days a week they usually worked from home: none, one or two, three or four, and five. Absence rates were adjusted for age, sex and occupation. The records do not show the cause of every absence, so we report only causes coded by the physician.

Back and neck. The share of consultations for back or neck pain was 21 percent among employees who worked five days a week at home and 14 percent among employees who never worked from home. Employees who had a height-adjustable desk and a proper chair from their employer had the same rate as office workers (14 percent), whatever their number of home days.

Sickness absence. Absence episodes per 100 employees were 41 for employees who worked one or two days a week at home and 49 for those who never did. For employees who worked five days a week at home the figure was 52, and most of the difference was in short episodes of one to three days. An absence counts as an episode when it lasts at least one working day.

Burnout. Physicians diagnosed burnout in 3.9 percent of patients who worked three or more days a week at home and in 2.7 percent of those who worked at the office. The gap was widest for employees who reported more than five unpaid hours a week, which suggests that long hours play a part. The numbers are small and the diagnosis is made after the patient comes forward.

Equipment. Only 38 percent of employees who worked from home three or more days a week had received any equipment from their employer, and 9 percent had received an assessment of their home workstation. Employers that paid for equipment saw fewer back and neck consultations among their staff than employers that did not. The rest had bought equipment themselves or used what they already owned.

Sleep. Employees who worked from home reported fewer sleep problems in consultations: 11 percent of those working one or two days a week at home mentioned poor sleep, against 16 percent of those who worked at the office. The difference disappeared among employees who worked five days a week at home. Sleep problems were recorded only when the patient raised them.

Isolation. Physicians noted social isolation as a contributing factor in 7 percent of consultations with employees under 30 who worked five days a week at home, and in 2 percent of those with employees under 30 who worked at the office. Numbers for hybrid employees were too small to report separately. Physicians recorded the factor in their own words, not on a scale.

Return to work. After long sickness absence, employees who could return gradually with some days at home regained full working hours on average three weeks earlier than employees who had to return to the office full time. Physicians regard home days as a useful part of a graded return plan, provided the employer agrees the hours in writing.

Commuting. Employees with a commute of more than 90 minutes a day had 12 percent more absence episodes than employees with a shorter commute, regardless of whether they worked from home. The records do not allow us to say whether fewer commuting days explain the lower absence of hybrid workers. The commuting time was reported by the patient and was not checked.

Recommendations. Employers should check the home workstation of everyone who works from home three or more days a week, agree rules on evening contact, and keep at least one shared office day for staff under 30. The service will repeat this analysis in 2026 with a larger sample. Employers may ask the service for a template for the workstation check.
